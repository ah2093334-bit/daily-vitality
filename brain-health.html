How Your Brain Works: A Simple Guide

By Wafadar • Updated August 2026 • 6 min read
💡 Quick Summary
  • The cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem each handle different core functions
  • 86 billion neurons communicate through electrical and chemical signals
  • Sleep, movement, learning, and social connection are the biggest protective habits

Your brain is the control center for everything you think, feel, and do — and it runs on roughly the same amount of power as a household lightbulb. Understanding its basic structure makes it much easier to understand what supports it.

The Main Parts of Your Brain

The cerebrum is the largest part, responsible for thinking, memory, language, and voluntary movement. It's divided into a left and right hemisphere, and four lobes: the frontal lobe (decision-making, personality), parietal lobe (sensory processing), temporal lobe (memory, hearing), and occipital lobe (vision).

The cerebellum, at the back of the brain, coordinates balance and movement. The brainstem connects the brain to the spinal cord and controls automatic functions like breathing and heart rate — things you never have to consciously think about.

How Brain Cells Communicate

Your brain contains around 86 billion neurons (nerve cells), which communicate using electrical and chemical signals. Every thought, memory, and movement happens because neurons are firing signals to one another across trillions of connections.

How Sleep Affects Your Brain

During deep sleep, your brain clears out waste proteins that build up during the day, and consolidates memories from short-term into long-term storage. Consistently poor sleep is linked to slower thinking and reduced focus over time.

📌
Important NoteSleep isn't downtime for the brain — it's an active cleanup and memory-filing process that happens almost nowhere else.

How Stress Affects Your Brain

Short bursts of stress can sharpen focus. But chronic, ongoing stress keeps a brain region called the amygdala (involved in fear and alertness) overactive, while shrinking activity in the prefrontal cortex, which handles planning and decision-making.

⚠️
Common MistakeTreating chronic stress as something to just "push through" ignores its measurable, cumulative effect on brain function.

Habits That Support Brain Health

None of this requires special supplements or brain-training apps — the habits that support brain health are largely the same ones that support the rest of your body.

✅ Helpful
  • Consistent sleep — 7 to 9 hours, at a regular time
  • Physical movement — increases blood flow to the brain
  • Learning new things — keeps neural pathways active
  • Social connection — genuinely protective over time
  • Managing chronic stress

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the brain really only use 10%?

No — this is a popular myth. Brain imaging shows virtually all regions of the brain are active over the course of a day, just not all simultaneously.

Can you grow new brain cells as an adult?

Yes, to a degree — a process called neurogenesis continues in certain brain regions throughout life, and exercise appears to support it.

What's the single best habit for brain health?

Consistent, adequate sleep is generally considered the single most protective daily habit, since so much repair and memory processing happens during it.
