Mouth Ulcers, Sores, and Oral Germs

Almost everyone gets a mouth sore at some point — and while they're usually harmless, understanding why they happen makes them far less alarming, and easier to prevent.

Canker Sores (The Small White Ulcers)

Canker sores are small, shallow ulcers that appear inside the mouth — on the inner cheeks, lips, or tongue. Unlike cold sores, they are not contagious. They're commonly triggered by minor injury (accidental bites), stress, or certain acidic foods.

Why the Inside of Your Mouth "Tears" or Breaks Down

The lining inside your mouth is thinner and more delicate than skin, and constantly exposed to friction, heat, and acidity from food. When this lining is irritated repeatedly — through hot food, sharp edges, or aggressive brushing — small sores can form.

Cold Sores (Different From Canker Sores)

Cold sores appear outside the mouth, usually on or around the lips, and are caused by the herpes simplex virus (HSV-1) — which means they are contagious. Once a person is exposed to the virus, it can remain dormant and resurface periodically, often triggered by stress or illness.

📌
Important NoteCanker sores and cold sores are commonly confused, but they have entirely different causes — one is not contagious, the other is.

What Germs Actually Live in Your Mouth

Your mouth naturally hosts hundreds of species of bacteria — most of them harmless or even helpful as part of your body's natural microbiome. Problems arise when harmful bacteria multiply due to poor hygiene, sugar, or dryness.

Simple Habits That Protect Your Mouth

✅ Helpful
  • Brushing gently, twice daily
  • Flossing once a day
  • Staying hydrated
  • Eating enough vitamin B12, iron, and folate
  • Letting hot food cool slightly before eating
❌ Best Avoided
  • Very acidic or spicy foods during a flare-up
  • Aggressive brushing
  • Chronic stress
  • Smoking or tobacco use
  • Ignoring a sharp or broken tooth edge
✅
Expert TipA soft-bristled toothbrush and gentle technique prevent far more mouth irritation than most people realize.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do mouth sores usually last?

Most canker sores heal on their own within one to two weeks; cold sores typically resolve in about the same timeframe once they appear.

Are mouth sores a sign of a vitamin deficiency?

Recurring canker sores can sometimes be linked to low B12, iron, or folate — worth mentioning to a doctor if they happen frequently.

When should I see a dentist about a mouth sore?

If a sore lasts more than two weeks, is unusually large or painful, or keeps recurring in the exact same spot, it's worth having checked.
