Your Body's Filtration System

Each kidney is a bean-shaped organ, roughly the size of a fist, that filters your entire blood supply dozens of times a day, removing waste, balancing fluid levels, and regulating key minerals — all without you ever noticing it happening.

PartWhat It Does
CortexThe outer layer, packed with over a million tiny filtering units called nephrons.
MedullaThe inner region, organized into pyramid-shaped structures that concentrate urine.
Renal PelvisA funnel-shaped chamber that collects filtered urine before it leaves the kidney.
UreterThe tube carrying urine from the kidney down to the bladder for storage.
Worth knowing: Your two kidneys filter your entire blood volume roughly 40 times a day — processing around 180 liters of fluid, of which about 1-2 liters actually leaves the body as urine.

What Puts Strain on the Kidneys

High blood pressure and diabetes are, by a wide margin, the two leading causes of long-term kidney damage, since both gradually harm the tiny blood vessels inside the nephrons. Dehydration, frequent use of certain pain medications, and untreated kidney stones can also add cumulative strain over time.

Keeping Kidneys Healthy

Consistent hydration keeps waste properly diluted and flowing, while managing blood pressure and blood sugar protects the delicate filtering vessels directly. Moderating sodium intake reduces the workload on the kidneys, and routine checkups with a simple urine or blood test can catch early kidney changes long before symptoms would ever appear.
