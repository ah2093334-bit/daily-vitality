How It Works

This tool estimates nutrient loss based on general scientific understanding of how different storage conditions affect common vitamins and minerals in produce. Factors like light, temperature, oxygen, and moisture all contribute to nutrient degradation. Our estimates are simplified for clarity and provide a practical guide, not precise laboratory measurements. Always prioritize fresh consumption for maximum nutrient intake.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes nutrient loss in produce?

Nutrient loss is primarily caused by exposure to heat, light, air (oxygen), and water. Enzymes naturally present in produce also contribute to degradation over time. For example, Vitamin C is highly susceptible to heat and oxidation.

Is freezing produce good for nutrient retention?

Yes, freezing is an excellent method for preserving nutrients, especially if produce is blanched first. Blanching (briefly boiling then chilling) deactivates enzymes that cause nutrient degradation. While some initial loss occurs during blanching, freezing then locks in most remaining nutrients for extended periods.

How can I minimize nutrient loss?

To minimize nutrient loss, consume produce as fresh as possible. Store items properly (e.g., refrigeration for most, cool dark places for others like potatoes). Avoid excessive washing or cutting before storage, and cook with minimal water and shorter times when possible.
