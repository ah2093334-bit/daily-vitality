How It Works

Our Produce Nutrient Loss Estimator provides an educated guess on how much nutritional value your fruits and vegetables might lose during storage. We consider the type of produce, its storage environment (like refrigeration or room temperature), and the duration of storage. Different nutrients degrade at different rates under various conditions.

The estimates are based on general scientific literature regarding nutrient stability. For example, Vitamin C is highly sensitive to heat, light, and oxygen, while minerals are generally more stable. This tool helps you understand the impact of your storage choices and offers practical tips to maximize the freshness and nutrient content of your food.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this estimator?

This tool provides an educational estimate based on general scientific understanding of nutrient degradation. Actual nutrient loss can vary significantly due to factors like initial ripeness, specific cultivar, temperature fluctuations, and light exposure. It's a guide, not a precise measurement.

Which nutrients are most affected by storage?

Water-soluble vitamins (like Vitamin C and B vitamins) are generally the most susceptible to degradation during storage, especially with exposure to heat, light, and oxygen. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and minerals tend to be more stable, though some loss can still occur over time.

Can I use this for cooked foods?

This estimator is primarily designed for fresh produce storage. Cooking methods themselves cause significant nutrient changes. While general principles of storage (e.g., refrigeration slows degradation) still apply to cooked foods, the specific estimates from this tool may not be directly applicable.
