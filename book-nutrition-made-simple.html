Nutrition Made Simple

Welcome

Nutrition Made Simple is a practical framework for everyday food decisions. It is not a restrictive diet and it does not prescribe one perfect way to eat.

Chapter 1 — Start with food groups

Build meals from a protein source, plant foods and a carbohydrate or fat source that fits the meal. Variety matters more than perfection.

Chapter 2 — Protein

Protein foods include eggs, dairy, fish, poultry, beans, lentils and tofu. Needs vary, so use individual guidance when medical conditions change protein requirements.

Chapter 3 — Fiber

Beans, lentils, vegetables, fruit, oats, whole grains, nuts and seeds are useful sources. Increase fiber gradually and include enough fluid.

Chapter 4 — Read labels

Compare serving size, protein, fiber, added sugar and sodium. Marketing claims can be helpful context but are not a substitute for the nutrition facts.

Chapter 5 — Packaged food is not automatically bad

Some packaged foods are practical and nutritious. Focus on the overall pattern and what the food contributes rather than using a single “processed” label as the entire decision.

Chapter 6 — Build your weekly system

Pick a small list of proteins, plants and easy staples. Rotate combinations. Keep a few emergency meals for busy days.

Closing

A sustainable food pattern should support your health, budget, culture, schedule and enjoyment. Keep the basics strong and change one thing at a time.
