Online Hearing Checks: How to Use Them Safely

Online listening checks can be useful prompts, but the device in your hand is not an audiometer. Use these checks as screening, not diagnosis.

What an online hearing check can do

A browser-based hearing check can ask whether you recognize spoken words, notice tones, or report everyday listening difficulties. That can be useful as a prompt to pay attention to your hearing, especially if you have been turning up the television, asking people to repeat themselves, or struggling in noisy rooms.

What it cannot measure reliably

Your phone, laptop, headphones and speakers are not calibrated clinical equipment. Their output changes with hardware, volume settings, fit, background noise and even the audio processing built into the device. For that reason, an ordinary website should not claim to measure your exact hearing threshold in decibels or give a medical “hearing percentage.”

Use audio checks conservatively

  • Start at a comfortable normal listening level.
  • Do not keep increasing the volume to “pass” a round.
  • Use a quiet room if possible.
  • If you use headphones, make sure they fit normally and do not blast a loud test tone.
  • Stop if the sound is uncomfortable.

When a professional hearing test makes sense

The National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders provides a practical self-check based on everyday listening difficulties and advises professional testing when hearing problems affect conversations or daily life. A clinician or audiologist can perform calibrated testing and interpret the pattern properly.
