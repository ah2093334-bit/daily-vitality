How it Works

This tool uses a simplified model to estimate safe sun exposure. It considers your skin type (how easily you burn), your geographical location (which affects sun intensity), and the time of year (which also impacts sun intensity). The goal is to find a balance for Vitamin D synthesis while minimizing sunburn risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Fitzpatrick Scale?

The Fitzpatrick scale is a way to classify your skin's reaction to sun exposure. It ranges from Type I (very fair, always burns) to Type VI (deeply pigmented, never burns). Knowing your skin type helps determine how much sun you can tolerate.

Why does location and time of year matter?

The intensity of the sun's UV rays varies greatly depending on your latitude and the season. Closer to the equator and during summer months, UV radiation is stronger, meaning you need less time in the sun to get Vitamin D and the risk of burning is higher.

How much Vitamin D do I need?

Recommended daily intake of Vitamin D varies by age and health status. While this tool focuses on safe exposure time, it's important to remember that individual Vitamin D needs and absorption can differ. Consult a healthcare provider for personalized advice.
