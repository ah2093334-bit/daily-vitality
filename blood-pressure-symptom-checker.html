Frequently Asked Questions

What is considered "high" blood pressure?

According to the American Heart Association (AHA), normal blood pressure is typically less than 120/80 mmHg. High blood pressure (hypertension) is generally defined as a systolic pressure of 130 mmHg or higher, or a diastolic pressure of 80 mmHg or higher. There are different stages of hypertension, and your doctor can provide a precise diagnosis.

Can low blood pressure cause symptoms?

Yes, low blood pressure (hypotension) can also cause symptoms such as dizziness, lightheadedness, fainting, blurred vision, fatigue, and nausea. While often less serious than high blood pressure, persistent low blood pressure or sudden drops should be discussed with a doctor, especially if it causes significant symptoms.

When should I see a doctor about my blood pressure?

You should consult a doctor if you experience any of the severe symptoms listed in this tool, or if you have persistent or concerning symptoms like frequent headaches, dizziness, or chest pain. Regular blood pressure checks are recommended for everyone, and your doctor can advise on the appropriate frequency based on your age and health history. If you have a sudden, severe headache, blurred vision, or chest pain, seek immediate medical attention.
