Biotin for Hair, Skin, and Nails: What the Evidence Actually Says

Biotin is a real nutrient with important metabolic functions, but the marketing around “hair, skin, and nails” often runs ahead of the evidence.

Daily Vitality Editorial Team•Updated September 22, 2026•Educational, non-diagnostic
About this article: This page is independently written for Daily Vitality and uses the public health sources listed below. It is not presented as a substitute for diagnosis or personalized medical care.

What biotin does

Biotin is a B vitamin that helps the body use carbohydrates, fats, and proteins for energy. It is present in many foods and most people obtain enough through a varied diet.

Food sources

Biotin is found in foods such as eggs, meat, fish, seeds, nuts, sweet potatoes, spinach, and broccoli. True deficiency is rare in the United States.

Hair, skin, and nail claims

Biotin deficiency can cause thinning hair, brittle nails, and skin changes. But for people who are not deficient, NIH notes that there is little scientific evidence that high-dose biotin supplements improve hair, skin, or nails.

The lab-test problem many people miss

High amounts of supplemental biotin can interfere with some laboratory tests and produce misleading results, including certain hormone tests. Tell your healthcare team about biotin before blood work.

Hair loss has many causes

Hair loss can relate to genetics, illness, stress, hormonal changes, medications, hairstyles, iron or other nutrient issues, and skin or autoimmune conditions. A supplement cannot identify the cause.

A more useful approach

Start with the basics: balanced food, gentle hair care, and attention to any sudden or patchy loss. If shedding is persistent, dramatic, or comes with scalp symptoms, a dermatologist can help identify the reason.
