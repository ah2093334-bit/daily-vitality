The Cyclosporiasis Outbreak: What's Behind the Surge in Stomach Illness

A parasite linked to contaminated lettuce has caused thousands of cases across the US this summer, with Michigan hit hardest. Here's what's actually happening, the symptoms to know, and how to protect your family.

What's Happening

A bigger outbreak than Michigan usually sees

Michigan typically logs around 50 cyclosporiasis cases a year. In 2026, that number passed 11,000, with cases concentrated in southeast Michigan and a connected multistate cluster reaching Ohio, West Virginia, and Kentucky. Investigators have linked a large share of the illnesses to iceberg lettuce, and a recall was issued for lettuce sourced from central Mexico.

Cyclosporiasis is caused by Cyclospora cayetanensis, a microscopic parasite that contaminates fresh produce or water. It's not spread person-to-person the way a cold is — it takes contaminated food or water to pass it on, which is why produce supply chains are the focus of the investigation.

Know the Signs

Symptoms of cyclosporiasis

Symptoms usually start about one to two weeks after eating contaminated food, and can drag on far longer than typical food poisoning if untreated:

💧 Watery diarrhea

Frequent, watery stools — the hallmark symptom, sometimes lasting weeks without treatment.

😩 Cramping & bloating

Stomach cramps, gas, bloating, and a general feeling of digestive distress.

🤢 Nausea & loss of appetite

Reduced appetite and nausea, sometimes with vomiting.

😴 Fatigue

Persistent tiredness, often out of proportion to how "sick" a person otherwise feels.

🌡️ Low-grade fever

A mild fever is common, though not everyone gets one.

⚖️ Weight loss

Extended illness without treatment can lead to noticeable weight loss from fluid and nutrient loss.

⚠️ When to see a doctor

See a doctor if diarrhea lasts more than a few days, if you can't keep fluids down, or if you notice signs of dehydration (very dark urine, dizziness, dry mouth). Ask specifically about testing for Cyclospora — it's often missed on standard stool panels unless requested.

Why It's Spreading

How the parasite gets from farm to plate

  1. 01
    Contaminated water or soil touches the crop during growing, often through irrigation.
  2. 02
    Leafy greens are hard to fully clean — the parasite's outer shell resists rinsing and standard sanitizers.
  3. 03
    Produce travels far and mixes — a single contaminated batch can reach many states and restaurants before anyone notices a pattern.
  4. 04
    The parasite needs time outside the body to become infectious, which is why it doesn't spread directly person-to-person.
Protect Your Household

Practical steps while the outbreak is active

Check recall notices. Follow FDA and your state health department for active produce recalls, and toss anything matching a recalled lot.

Rinse thoroughly, but know the limits. Washing removes dirt and some contamination, but Cyclospora's tough outer shell means rinsing alone can't guarantee safety during an active outbreak — cooking greens when possible reduces risk further.

Buy from traceable sources. Farmers markets and stores that can name their supplier make it easier to know if your produce is affected if a recall is announced.

Stay hydrated if you do get sick. Watery diarrhea can dehydrate you quickly — oral rehydration solutions or diluted electrolyte drinks help more than plain water alone.

Ask for the right test. If you're sick with prolonged diarrhea, tell your doctor about the outbreak so they can specifically test for Cyclospora.

Good to Know

Frequently asked questions

Is cyclosporiasis contagious from person to person?

No — it requires the parasite to mature outside the body first, so it spreads through contaminated food or water, not casual contact.

How long does the illness last?

Untreated, symptoms can persist for a month or longer, though most people improve with appropriate antibiotic treatment.

Is all lettuce affected?

No — investigators are tracing specific supply chains. Following official recall notices is the most reliable way to know if a product is implicated.

Who is most at risk of severe illness?

People with underlying health conditions and those who become significantly dehydrated face higher risk of complications.

This article summarizes public health guidance for general education and does not replace medical advice. If you suspect you have cyclosporiasis or are severely dehydrated, contact a doctor.
