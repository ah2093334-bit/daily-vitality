How It Works

This Sleep Apnea Risk Quiz uses a combination of common risk factors and symptoms to provide an estimated risk level. It considers your age, body mass index (BMI), and answers to questions about snoring, daytime fatigue, observed breathing pauses, and high blood pressure. While not a diagnostic tool, it can help you understand potential indicators and guide discussions with your doctor about sleep health. The scoring is based on established clinical indicators often used in preliminary screening for sleep apnea.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is sleep apnea?

Sleep apnea is a serious sleep disorder in which breathing repeatedly stops and starts. If you snore loudly and feel tired even after a full night's sleep, you might have sleep apnea. There are several types, but the most common is obstructive sleep apnea, which occurs when throat muscles relax and block the airway during sleep.

What are the main symptoms of sleep apnea?

Key symptoms include loud snoring, episodes in which you stop breathing during sleep (reported by another person), gasping for air during sleep, awakening with a dry mouth, morning headache, insomnia, excessive daytime sleepiness (hypersomnia), difficulty paying attention while awake, and irritability.

What should I do if my risk is high?

If your estimated risk is high, it's strongly recommended to schedule an appointment with your doctor. They can evaluate your symptoms, conduct a physical examination, and may recommend a sleep study (polysomnography) to accurately diagnose sleep apnea and discuss appropriate treatment options. Early diagnosis and treatment are important for managing the condition and preventing potential health complications.
