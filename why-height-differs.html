Why Height Differs: Growth Plates, Genetics, and What Determines It

Daily Vitality · 5 min read

Height feels like a fixed trait, but it's actually the result of a precise biological process happening at the ends of your bones during childhood.

← scroll for more →

Where Height Actually Comes From

Height is determined almost entirely by how much your long bones — particularly in the legs and spine — lengthen during childhood and adolescence. That lengthening happens at growth plates: thin layers of cartilage near each end of a bone that produce new bone tissue as you grow.

PartWhat It Does
Growth PlateA cartilage layer where new bone cells form, allowing a bone to lengthen over time.
EpiphysisThe end section of a long bone, where growth plates are located.
DiaphysisThe long shaft of the bone between the two growth plates.
Growth HormoneA hormone from the pituitary gland that signals growth plates to remain active.
Worth knowing: Growth plates typically fuse and close by around age 18, at which point a person's height becomes essentially fixed for the rest of adult life, regardless of diet or exercise afterward.

Why Genetics Dominates

Roughly 80% of height variation between people is estimated to come from inherited genetics — hundreds of different genes each contribute a small effect, which is why height tends to run strongly in families and is difficult to predict from any single trait.

What the Remaining Factors Actually Are

The other roughly 20% comes largely from childhood nutrition and health. Adequate protein, calcium, and overall calorie intake during growing years support growth plates in reaching their full genetic potential, while chronic illness, malnutrition, or hormone deficiencies during childhood can measurably limit final height. None of these factors have any effect once growth plates have already closed in adulthood.
