BMR vs. TDEE: What These Energy Estimates Actually Mean

Daily Vitality Editorial Team · Updated Oct 2, 2026

Quick answer

BMR estimates the energy your body uses at rest; TDEE adds an activity estimate. Both are starting points, not precise prescriptions.

What BMR means

Basal or resting energy equations estimate how much energy your body may use at rest. The Mifflin–St Jeor equation uses age, height, weight and sex in a predictive formula; it is still an estimate, not direct calorimetry.

How TDEE is estimated

TDEE usually takes a resting-energy estimate and applies an activity factor. That makes it useful for rough planning, but daily movement, body composition, health conditions and measurement error can shift the real number.

How to use the number safely

Treat the result as a conversation starter. If weight, appetite, pregnancy, an eating disorder, chronic illness or medication affects your needs, individualized guidance is more appropriate than an online estimate.
