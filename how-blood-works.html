How Blood Works: Understanding Blood Cells and Common Blood Disorders

Daily Vitality · 6 min read

Blood looks like a single red liquid — but it's actually a carefully balanced mix of cells and fluid, each with a distinct job to keep you alive.

What's Actually In Your Blood

Blood is roughly 55% plasma — a straw-colored liquid made mostly of water, proteins, and nutrients — with the remainder made up of three types of specialized cells suspended within it, each with a very different job.

PartWhat It Does
Red Blood CellsCarry oxygen from your lungs to every tissue in your body, using a protein called hemoglobin.
White Blood CellsYour immune system's front line, identifying and fighting off infections and foreign invaders.
PlateletsSmall cell fragments that clump together to stop bleeding when a blood vessel is damaged.
PlasmaThe liquid carrier that transports cells, nutrients, hormones, and waste products throughout the body.
Worth knowing: A single drop of blood, smaller than this letter ‘o’, contains around 5 million red blood cells — which is roughly why blood looks so uniformly red despite being mostly clear liquid.

How Blood Problems Actually Show Up

Anemia occurs when red blood cell count or hemoglobin drops too low, leaving tissues short on oxygen — causing fatigue and pale skin. Clotting disorders affect how well platelets and clotting proteins work, leading to either excessive bleeding or dangerous clots. Infections and some cancers can cause white blood cell counts to spike or crash. Most of these are first caught through a routine complete blood count (CBC), one of the most common blood tests ordered.

What Supports Healthy Blood

Iron-rich foods support red blood cell production, while adequate hydration keeps plasma volume and circulation running smoothly. Regular movement improves circulation efficiency, and avoiding smoking protects blood vessels from the damage that makes clotting problems more likely over time.
